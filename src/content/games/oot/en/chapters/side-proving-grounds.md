> **Order:** as an adult, once the Gerudo stop chasing you, that is after you free the four carpenters in Gerudo’s Fortress and get their membership card (Gerudo’s Card). Come with almost all your gear, ideally after the Spirit Temple.

The Gerudo Proving Grounds (the old Gerudo Training Ground) are an optional mini-dungeon in Gerudo’s Fortress. There is no boss: it is a series of trial rooms, and the prize is the **Ice Arrows**.

## What changes in the remake

- The Silver Rupees you had to collect in some rooms are now **silver gems**: they are not Rupees and, once you collect them all, they fly into the door’s lock.
- The remake removed most puzzle countdowns. In the original game several rooms here had a time limit and threw you out of the room when it ran out; what remains is not documented.
- The layout of the remake’s rooms is not documented yet. What follows is the original game’s structure (not yet confirmed for the remake).

## Layout (original game)

- At the entrance a Gerudo charges you to go in (10 Rupees in the original game).
- The entrance room has three doors: north, the Treasure Room with the Ice Arrows; west, the stone garden; south, the Dinolfos room.
- The Treasure Room is split into fenced cells with locked doors. Each trial room gives you a small key; you need **7 keys** to reach the Ice Arrows chest, and two more open the remaining cells, which only hold Rupees.
- One cell has a fake ceiling: the Lenses of Truth show you the way through to an isolated chest.

## What to bring

In the original game you use the Longshot, the Hover Boots, the Iron Boots, the Silver Gauntlets, the Megaton Hammer, the Lenses of Truth, the Bow, bombs and the Song of Time (list not yet confirmed for the remake). Put the Lenses on with D-pad ↑ and the boots with D-pad ↓, and bring plenty of magic and a fairy in a bottle.

> 💡 If you leave a room halfway, in the original game its enemies and gems reset; rooms you have cleared stay solved for good.

## The Ice Arrows

They freeze enemies for a few seconds and, against enemies that can be frozen, deal twice the damage of a normal arrow (original-game facts). They use magic like the Fire Arrows. They are not required, but the statues in the Hall of Fallen Foes hint at how to use them: Volvagia’s fire breath stops if you freeze its mouth, and Bongo Bongo can’t drum as well if you freeze its hands.
